Research

One question, four themes

How can mobile systems stay connected, compute efficiently, and act autonomously in the real world? Each theme below answers one part of this question. Most projects draw on two or more themes and end with a working prototype whose system-level performance we measure on real hardware.

Research framework Sense → Connect → Compute → Reason → Act Platforms: UAVs · vehicles · robots · edge devices

Wireless & Intelligent Networks

Selected: IEEE TVT 2024 · ICUAS 2022 · ISCMI 2024

How can mobile systems stay reliably connected when the devices, and sometimes the network itself, are moving?

Mobile systems are only as capable as their links. We design, prototype, and evaluate wireless networks for drones, vehicles, and other platforms that move, from the radio up to the network architecture. A recurring focus is system-level performance: whether radio hardware, software stacks, and embedded computers work together reliably. Dr. Zhang's doctoral work put a working LTE base station on a UAV.

  • System-level performance evaluation of SDR-based cellular stacks on embedded platforms, including hardware/software compatibility
  • Software-defined radio (SDR) LTE/5G systems for aerial platforms
  • Open RAN (O-RAN) architecture and AI-driven network automation
  • Sub-6 GHz-assisted mmWave links and V2X scheduling that weighs data importance and freshness
  • Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) for 6G
  • Network traffic forecasting with graph neural networks

PlatformsSoftware-defined radios · LTE/5G and O-RAN software stacks · UAV-mounted radios · V2X links

Edge Intelligence & Computing

Selected: IEEE TVT 2024 · GLOBECOM 2021

How much computing can a mobile system carry, and how should work be split between the device, the edge, and the network?

How much intelligence fits on a drone, a vehicle, or a base station? We study the computing side of AI-native networks and embedded AI, from GPU-accelerated platforms down to microcontrollers.

  • AI-RAN: running radio access network workloads on GPU computing platforms for 6G
  • Task distribution and instruction-level optimization on airborne and embedded computers
  • Performance of microcontroller-based IoT systems
  • Resource allocation between communication and computing tasks
  • Federated and coded learning across heterogeneous devices

PlatformsNVIDIA GPU computing platforms · embedded airborne computers · microcontrollers

AI for Mobile Systems

Selected: MLMI 2024 · ICACTE 2024 · ISCMI 2024 · MLBDBI 2024

How can AI, including large language models, help mobile systems perceive, decide, and adapt reliably?

AI helps mobile systems perceive, decide, and adapt. We develop learning-based perception and decision-making for autonomous platforms, and we test where large language models can be trusted as a reasoning layer for networks and devices. We also apply these tools to widen access, starting with mixed-reality tools that help deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) adults communicate with hearing people.

  • Lightweight aerial perception with YOLO, CNNs, RNNs, and bird's-eye-view datasets
  • AI decision-making for autonomous driving and intersection management
  • Systematic evaluation of open and commercial LLMs, and prompt design for engineering tasks
  • LLM agents that monitor and configure networks and devices
  • Spatio-temporal learning for traffic and mobility prediction
  • Human-centered mobile AI: mixed-reality environments and real-time sign and speech understanding for DHH communication
  • AI tools for engineering education

PlatformsYOLO · OpenCV · CNNs and RNNs · open-weight and commercial LLMs · mixed-reality devices

Cyber-Physical Systems

Selected: ICUAS 2022 · ISCMI 2024

How do sensing, communication, computing, and control work together so that autonomous platforms stay safe and reliable?

Cyber-physical systems tie computing and communication to physical motion. Their sensing, networking, and control loops must be reliable, secure, and fast enough for the real world. We model these loops and validate them on physical platforms.

  • UAV navigation: combining perception, communication, and control for autonomous flight
  • Sensor-based control combining classical control (PID) with learned models
  • Networked control and timing under wireless uncertainty
  • Resilient, secure CPS frameworks built on O-RAN
  • Hardware-in-the-loop testing with ROS and physical drones

PlatformsROS · physical drones · hardware-in-the-loop testbeds · PID and learning-based controllers
